# 0.2 The Packet Tracer Tour

> [!abstract] Why this matters
> You'll use the same few parts of Packet Tracer in every lab. Learning them now means the labs are about networking, not about hunting for buttons.

## The parts of the window

- **The workspace** is the big white area. You put devices here.
- **The device box** is at the bottom left. It has groups: Network Devices, End Devices, Connections, and more.
- **Realtime and Simulation** are two tabs at the bottom right. Realtime means the network runs normally. Simulation means time stops, and you step through each packet one at a time. A packet is one small piece of data sent across a network.
- **The inspect tool** is the magnifying glass on the right. Click a device with it to see its tables, like the MAC address table.

## The devices you'll use most

| Device | Packet Tracer name | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Router | 2911 | Network Devices → Routers |
| Switch | 2960-24TT | Network Devices → Switches |
| Multilayer switch | 3560-24PS | Network Devices → Switches |
| PC | PC-PT | End Devices |
| Server | Server-PT | End Devices |

A multilayer switch is a switch that can also route. You'll meet it in Phase 2.

## Cables

In the Connections group, the lightning bolt picks the right cable for you. It's quick, but you learn more by picking yourself.

- **Copper Straight-Through** connects different kinds of device. For example, a PC to a switch.
- **Copper Cross-Over** connects the same kind of device. For example, a switch to a switch.
- **Console** is the light blue cable. It connects a PC's serial port to a device's console port, so you can type commands into it.

Modern devices fix the wrong cable for you. This is called **Auto-MDIX**. The exam still expects you to know which cable is correct.

## Lights on the cable

Each end of a cable has a small light.

- **Green** means the link is up.
- **Orange** means the switch port is starting up, or is blocked by spanning tree. Wait up to 30 seconds.
- **Red** means the link is down. Usually the interface is shut down, or the cable is in the wrong port.

## Lab: two PCs and a switch

1. Put one **2960** switch and two **PC-PT** PCs in the workspace.
2. Connect PC0's FastEthernet0 port to the switch's Fa0/1 port with a **Copper Straight-Through** cable.
3. Connect PC1 to Fa0/2 in the same way. Wait until all the lights are green.
4. Click PC0 → **Desktop** → **IP Configuration**. Type the IP address `192.168.1.10` and the subnet mask `255.255.255.0`.
5. Give PC1 the address `192.168.1.11` with the same mask.
6. On PC0, open **Desktop** → **Command Prompt** and type `ping 192.168.1.11`.
7. You should see four replies. The first may time out while the PCs find each other. That's normal.
8. Click **Simulation**, then ping again. Click **Play** or **Capture / Forward**. Watch the envelopes move. Click an envelope to see what's inside it.
9. Save the lab as `0.2 Tour.pkt`.

> [!sith] The dark side
> Leaving Packet Tracer in Simulation mode, then wondering why nothing works. In Simulation, packets only move when you press the buttons. If a ping just waits, check the bottom right corner and switch back to Realtime.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- Which cable connects a PC to a switch, and which connects a switch to a switch?
> A straight-through cable connects different kinds of device, like a PC to a switch. A cross-over cable connects the same kind of device, like a switch to a switch.

> [!question]- What does Auto-MDIX do?
> Auto-MDIX lets a port detect the wrong cable type and fix it for you, so a straight-through cable works even where a cross-over cable is expected.

> [!question]- What is the difference between Realtime and Simulation mode in Packet Tracer?
> In Realtime mode the network runs normally. In Simulation mode time stops, and you step through each packet one at a time to see what it contains and where it goes.

> [!question]- What does a red light on a cable end usually mean?
> A red light means the link is down. Usually the interface is shut down, or the cable is plugged into the wrong port.
