# 2.05 EtherChannel

> [!abstract] Why this matters
> STP blocks extra links, so two cables between switches only give you the speed of one. EtherChannel bundles several links into one logical link. STP sees one link, so nothing is blocked, and you get the speed of all of them.

## The idea

An **EtherChannel** joins up to 8 physical links into one logical link called a **port channel**. If one cable fails, the others keep working.

All links in the bundle must match: the same speed, duplex, VLAN mode, allowed VLANs and native VLAN.

## The two protocols, and "on"

| Protocol | Standard? | Modes | Works when |
|---|---|---|---|
| LACP | Yes, IEEE 802.3ad | `active`, `passive` | At least one end is active |
| PAgP | Cisco only | `desirable`, `auto` | At least one end is desirable |
| None | | `on` | Both ends are on |

So: active with passive works, passive with passive doesn't. Desirable with auto works, auto with auto doesn't.

Use LACP. It's the standard, and it works with other makers' switches.

## The commands

```text
interface range g0/1 - 2
 channel-group 1 mode active
 exit
interface port-channel 1
 switchport mode trunk
 end
show etherchannel summary
```

After the bundle is made, change settings on the **port-channel** interface. They copy down to the member ports.

In `show etherchannel summary`:

- `SU` next to the port channel means it's a Layer 2 channel and in use. That's good.
- `P` next to a member port means it's bundled. That's good.
- `D` means down. `s` means suspended, usually because a setting doesn't match.

## Load balancing

Each conversation goes over one link in the bundle. The switch picks the link using the MAC or IP addresses. It doesn't split one conversation across links. You can change how it picks with `port-channel load-balance src-dst-ip`.

## Lab

1. Build two 2960 switches. Connect them with **two** cross-over cables, G0/1 to G0/1 and G0/2 to G0/2.
2. Wait. One link goes orange. STP has blocked it.
3. On both switches, type the commands above.
4. Run `show etherchannel summary`. You should see `Po1(SU)` with `Gig0/1(P)` and `Gig0/2(P)`.
5. Both lights are green now. Run `show spanning-tree`. STP sees only Po1.
6. Put a PC on each switch in the same network, and ping continuously with `ping -t`. Delete one of the two cables. The pings keep working.
7. Try it with PAgP: `channel-group 1 mode desirable` on one side and `auto` on the other. Then try `auto` on both sides, and see that no channel forms.

> [!sith] The dark side
> A setting that doesn't match on one member port. For example, one port is in a different VLAN. That port is suspended, and you lose bandwidth without noticing. Make every member port the same before bundling, and check `show etherchannel summary` afterwards.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- What does EtherChannel do?
> EtherChannel joins several physical links between two switches into one logical link, so STP doesn't block them and you get the speed of all of them together.

> [!question]- What are the LACP modes, and which pairs form a channel?
> The LACP modes are active and passive. Active with active, and active with passive, form a channel. Passive with passive does not.

> [!question]- What are the PAgP modes, and which pair will not form a channel?
> The PAgP modes are desirable and auto. Auto with auto will not form a channel, because both ends wait for the other.

> [!question]- Which EtherChannel protocol is an open standard?
> LACP, which is IEEE 802.3ad. PAgP only works on Cisco devices.

> [!question]- Which command shows whether an EtherChannel is up and which ports are in it?
> show etherchannel summary.

> [!question]- Does one conversation use every link in an EtherChannel at once?
> No. Each conversation uses one link, chosen from its addresses. Different conversations are spread over different links.
