# 4.05 QoS Basics

> [!abstract] Why this matters
> When a link is busy, something has to wait. QoS decides what waits. Without it, a big file download can make phone calls break up. The exam asks you to explain how QoS works, not to set it up in detail.

## Why some traffic needs care

QoS means quality of service. Four things hurt traffic:

- **Bandwidth**: how much data the link can carry.
- **Delay**, or latency: how long a packet takes to arrive.
- **Jitter**: how much the delay changes from packet to packet.
- **Loss**: packets that never arrive.

Voice is the most sensitive. Good voice needs one-way delay under **150 ms**, jitter under **30 ms**, and loss under **1%**.

## The steps of QoS

1. **Classification** sorts packets into groups. For example, voice, video, business apps and everything else.
2. **Marking** writes the group into the packet header, so later devices don't need to sort it again.
3. **Queuing** puts packets into separate queues when the link is busy, and decides which queue is sent first.
4. **Policing** drops, or re-marks, traffic that goes over a set rate.
5. **Shaping** holds traffic over the rate in a queue and sends it later. This smooths it out instead of dropping it.

### Where the marks go

| Field | Where | Size |
|---|---|---|
| DSCP | The IP header | 6 bits, 0 to 63 |
| CoS, also called PCP | The 802.1Q tag | 3 bits, 0 to 7 |

Common DSCP values:

- **EF**, expedited forwarding, is **DSCP 46**. Used for voice.
- **AF** values, assured forwarding, like AF41 for video. AF classes have drop levels: AF41, AF42, AF43. A higher last digit is dropped first.
- **CS** values, class selector, like CS3 for call signalling.
- **0**, or default, is best effort.

### Trust boundary

The **trust boundary** is where the network starts believing the marks it receives. Usually that's the access switch, or the IP phone. A PC could mark all its traffic as voice to get ahead, so marks from PCs aren't trusted.

### Queuing

- **CBWFQ**, class-based weighted fair queuing, gives each class a share of the bandwidth.
- **LLQ**, low latency queuing, adds a **priority queue** that's always sent first. Voice goes there. The priority queue has a limit, so it can't starve everything else.

### Congestion avoidance

When a queue fills up, the end of it is dropped. That's **tail drop**. **WRED**, weighted random early detection, drops a few low-priority packets early instead. TCP senders then slow down before the queue is full.

## Lab

Packet Tracer has limited QoS commands. Instead:

1. Open any lab with an IP phone, or add one: **IP Phone 7960** in End Devices. Connect it to a 3560 switch port with a voice VLAN.
2. In **Simulation** mode, look at a packet from the phone. Find the **DSCP** or **TOS** field in the IP header.
3. On paper, sort these into classes, give each a DSCP value, and say which goes in the priority queue: voice calls, video meetings, email, a backup job, web browsing.

> [!sith] The dark side
> Thinking QoS makes a link faster. It doesn't add bandwidth. It only decides who waits when the link is full. If a link is always full, the real fix is more bandwidth.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- What are bandwidth, delay, jitter and loss?
> Bandwidth is how much a link can carry. Delay is how long a packet takes to arrive. Jitter is how much the delay changes between packets. Loss is packets that never arrive.

> [!question]- What are the limits for good voice quality?
> One-way delay under 150 milliseconds, jitter under 30 milliseconds, and loss under 1 percent.

> [!question]- What is the difference between policing and shaping?
> Policing drops or re-marks traffic over the rate straight away. Shaping holds the extra traffic in a queue and sends it later.

> [!question]- What DSCP value is used for voice?
> EF, expedited forwarding, which is DSCP 46.

> [!question]- What is a trust boundary?
> The trust boundary is the point in the network where devices start believing the QoS marks on packets, usually at the access switch or the IP phone.

> [!question]- What does LLQ add to CBWFQ?
> LLQ adds a strict priority queue that is always sent first, used for voice, with a limit so it can't starve the other queues.

> [!question]- Where are DSCP and CoS marks carried?
> DSCP is 6 bits in the IP header. CoS is 3 bits in the 802.1Q tag of an Ethernet frame.
