# 5.06 Security Concepts and VPNs

> [!abstract] Why this matters
> The exam asks you to name threats and say how to defend against them. It also asks about VPNs, which protect traffic across the internet. This note gives you the words to use.

## The key words

- A **vulnerability** is a weakness. For example, an old IOS version with a known bug.
- An **exploit** is a way to use that weakness.
- A **threat** is someone or something that could use the exploit.
- **Mitigation** is what you do to reduce the risk.

The **CIA triad** sums up what security protects:

- **Confidentiality**: only the right people can read the data.
- **Integrity**: the data isn't changed.
- **Availability**: the data and services are there when needed.

## Common attacks

| Attack | What it is | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing | A fake email or message that tricks someone into giving a password or opening a bad file | User training, email filtering, MFA |
| Malware | Harmful software: viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware | Antivirus, updates, backups |
| DoS and DDoS | Flooding a service until it stops. DDoS uses many machines at once. | Rate limiting, filtering by the internet provider |
| Spoofing | Pretending to be another address, like IP, MAC or ARP | DHCP snooping, DAI, ACLs |
| Man-in-the-middle | Sitting between two devices and reading or changing traffic | Encryption, DAI |
| Reconnaissance | Gathering information before an attack, like a port scan | Turn off unused services and CDP on outside ports |
| Password attacks | Guessing, or trying a long list of passwords | Long passwords, MFA, login block-for |
| Social engineering | Tricking people rather than computers | Training and clear procedures |

## A security programme

Technology isn't enough. A good **security programme** also has:

- **User awareness**: teaching everyone to spot phishing.
- **Training**: deeper lessons for staff who look after systems.
- **Physical access control**: locked rooms, badges and cameras. Someone in the server room can bypass most other defences.

## VPNs

A **VPN**, virtual private network, makes a private, encrypted tunnel across a public network like the internet.

- A **site-to-site VPN** joins two offices. The routers or firewalls build the tunnel. The users don't need to do anything.
- A **remote-access VPN** joins one person's laptop to the office. They run a VPN program on the laptop, like Cisco Secure Client.

**IPsec** is the usual protocol for site-to-site VPNs. It gives:

- **Confidentiality**, by encryption, for example with AES.
- **Integrity**, by a hash, for example with SHA, so changes are spotted.
- **Authentication**, with a pre-shared key or certificates.

Remote-access VPNs often use **TLS**, the same encryption used by HTTPS websites.

> [!sith] The dark side
> Thinking security is only firewalls and ACLs. Many real attacks start with one person clicking a link in an email. A network with strong ACLs and no user training is still easy to break into. That's why the exam includes user awareness and physical security.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- What is the difference between a vulnerability, an exploit and a threat?
> A vulnerability is a weakness. An exploit is a way to use that weakness. A threat is someone or something that could carry out the exploit.

> [!question]- What do the three parts of the CIA triad mean?
> Confidentiality means only the right people can read the data. Integrity means the data is not changed. Availability means the data and services are there when they are needed.

> [!question]- What is phishing, and how do you defend against it?
> Phishing is a fake message that tricks someone into giving away a password or opening a harmful file. Defences are user training, email filtering and multi-factor authentication.

> [!question]- What is the difference between a site-to-site VPN and a remote-access VPN?
> A site-to-site VPN joins two whole networks, and the routers or firewalls build the tunnel. A remote-access VPN joins one user's device to a network, using a VPN program on that device.

> [!question]- What three things does IPsec provide?
> Confidentiality through encryption, integrity through hashing, and authentication of the two ends.

> [!question]- What are the three parts of a security programme besides technology?
> User awareness, training for staff, and physical access control.

> [!question]- What is the difference between a DoS and a DDoS attack?
> A DoS attack floods a service from one source until it stops working. A DDoS attack does the same from many sources at once, which makes it much harder to block.
